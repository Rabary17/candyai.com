**The 30-second answer**
An AI companion robot is a physical device with a chat AI inside, but most people who search for one end up better served by an app. Pick a robot only if you want something on your desk that talks back, and check its subscription and microphone settings first.

</div>

The phrase "AI companion robot" now covers two very different things. Some are small physical devices with a face, a speaker and a microphone. Others are chat apps that borrow the word "robot" for marketing. Knowing which one you are looking at saves money and disappointment.

## What is it, exactly?

An AI companion robot is a physical gadget that uses a language model (the software that writes replies in natural sentences) to hold conversations, usually by voice.

<div class="exemple">

It is late on a Tuesday. You come home, and the small device on your shelf lights up. You ask how it handled the day's reminders, and it answers aloud. Then you mention a stressful meeting, and it asks a follow-up question. The same chat could also happen on your phone, without the hardware.

</div>

Most of these devices send your voice to a remote server to generate the reply. Without internet, many of them do very little. For a deeper look at what the hardware can and cannot do, read [AI Companion Robots: What They Can Really Do in 2026](/ai-companion/robot/).

## Which one should you pick?

Your choice comes down to **robot or app**. Each has real strengths, and neither is better for everyone.

A **physical device** suits you if you like having an object in the room. It can react to your voice, sit on a desk and feel more present than a screen. The trade-offs are the upfront cost, limited conversation depth and a microphone that stays in your home.

A **chat app** suits you if you want longer conversations, memory of past chats and a character you can shape. It lives on your phone, so you can use it anywhere. Many apps also ask for a monthly plan, and some count usage in tokens (credits spent on messages, images or calls). For example, Candy AI bills through EverAI Limited in Malta and uses a token system alongside its plans. See [Candy AI Pricing: What You Pay, Plan by Plan, Token by Token](/candy-ai/pricing/) before you commit.

A short way to decide:

1. Do you want to talk out loud to something in your room? Look at a robot.
2. Do you want a long, evolving chat with a character? Look at an app.
3. Do you want to try before spending? Start with a free app tier.

<div class="attention">

Check three things before you pay for any companion, robot or app. First, find out whether the plan renews automatically and how to cancel it. Second, read what the device or app does with voice recordings and chat logs. Third, look at the company name on your bank statement, since it may differ from the brand name. Keep screenshots of your plan and renewal date.

</div>

## Mistakes to avoid

- **Buying on looks.** A cute shell says nothing about the quality of the conversation inside.
- Ignoring the **subscription**. Some devices lose features if you stop paying a monthly fee.
- Leaving the **microphone** always on without reading the privacy settings.
- Treating marketing claims about "feelings" as fact. The software predicts text. It does not feel.
- Expecting a robot to do **chores**. Most companion devices talk and react. They do not carry or clean.
- Downloading unofficial app files from random sites. Use the official app stores or the company's own site.

## Key terms

<div class="lexique">

**Language model**: software trained on large amounts of text that predicts and writes replies.

**Token**: a unit of credit that some services spend when you send a message, make an image or place a call.

**Cloud processing**: sending your voice or text to a remote server, which writes the reply and sends it back.

**Memory**: a feature that lets the companion recall details from earlier chats.

**Auto-renewal**: a billing setting that charges you again at the end of each period unless you cancel.

</div>

## Frequently asked questions

### Is an AI companion robot the same as an AI girlfriend?

Not quite. A robot is a physical device. An AI girlfriend is usually a chat app with a romantic character. The terms overlap in marketing, so check what you actually receive. [AI Robot Girlfriend: What Exists Today, and What Is Hype](/ai-girlfriend/robot/) sorts out the difference.

### Does a companion robot work without internet?

Often not well. Many devices rely on cloud processing for their replies. Without a connection, they may only handle simple, preset responses. Check the product page for offline features before buying.

### Is a robot safer for privacy than an app?

Not automatically. A device with a microphone can collect voice data, and apps collect chat logs. Read the privacy policy, find the delete-data option and limit what you share in either case.

### Can I try the experience before buying hardware?

Yes. A free app tier lets you test whether talking to an AI suits you. You can then decide if a physical device adds anything for you.
